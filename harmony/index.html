Color is the subject here,
not the decoration.

Most design tools treat color as a setting: a small square you click, buried in an inspector, on the way to doing something else. Harmony Palette is the opposite kind of program. Color is the whole screen, the interface is loud on purpose, and the point is to spend time with it rather than get past it.

That is a design decision, not an accident. The thick outlines and flat blocks are there because a color needs a hard edge to be judged against. Put a swatch on a soft gradient and you cannot tell what you are looking at. Put it in a black box next to another black box and you can.

two wheels, because
they disagree

Ask a screen what the opposite of red is and it says cyan. Ask a painter and they say green. Both are right, and they are answering different questions: one is how light mixes, the other is the wheel taught in art schools and refined by Johannes Itten at the Bauhaus.

This matters more than it sounds. A triad that looks evenly weighted on the RGB wheel can come out lopsided in RYB, and a palette built for print behaves differently from one built for a screen. Harmony Palette gives you both models and lets you flip between them on the same color, so you can see the disagreement instead of picking a side by accident.

Complementary

Two colors facing each other. The strongest contrast available, and the easiest to overdo.

Analogous

Three neighbours. Quiet and cohesive, which is why so much brand work lives here.

Monochromatic

One hue, worked through its shades and tints. Hard to get wrong.

Triadic

Three evenly spaced. Vivid and balanced at the same time, which is a rare combination.

Split-complementary

A base plus the two neighbours of its opposite. Nearly the same punch, far less tension.

Square & rectangular

Four colors, evenly or in pairs. Enough range for a whole system.

Compound

Complementary and analogous relationships braided together. Complex, and worth the trouble.

Eight in total

Four in the free version. The other four, and the curated library, come with Pro.

the accessibility part
you were going to skip

Contrast checking usually lives in a separate browser tab, gets done at the end, and gets skipped when the deadline moves. It is in here, next to the colors, while you are still choosing them.

Harmony Palette reports the WCAG 2.1 contrast ratio for any foreground and background pair and grades it AA and AAA, separately for normal and large text, because the thresholds are different: 4.5:1 and 3:1. When a pair falls short it can adjust the last color you touched until it clears the bar, so the fix takes a tap instead of a spreadsheet.

It also shows you the palette as someone else sees it. Protanopia, deuteranopia, tritanopia and achromatopsia, applied to a single color or to a whole saved palette, so you find out that your success green and your error red are the same swatch to a large number of people before you ship it rather than after.

questions

What is the difference between the RGB and RYB color wheels?

RGB is how a screen mixes light, so the complement of red is cyan. RYB is the wheel taught in art schools and refined by Johannes Itten at the Bauhaus, where the complement of red is green. Neither is wrong; they answer different questions. A palette that looks balanced on one can look lopsided on the other, so you get both and can switch between them on the same color.

Does Harmony Palette check color contrast for accessibility?

Yes. It reports the WCAG 2.1 contrast ratio for any foreground and background pair and grades it AA and AAA for normal and large text. If a pair falls short it can adjust the last color you touched until it reaches 4.5:1. It also shows how a palette reads under protanopia, deuteranopia, tritanopia and achromatopsia.

Can I export a palette as code?

Yes. Pro exports ready-to-paste snippets for SwiftUI, UIKit, CSS custom properties and Tailwind, plus multi-page PDF and SVG. The free tier gives you HEX, RGB, HSL and HSV values to copy by hand.

Is Harmony Palette free?

The free tier is a real tool rather than a demo: the full wheel, four harmony types, up to fifteen saved palettes and color extraction from photos. Pro adds the other four harmonies, unlimited palettes, the curated library, the accessibility suite and the export formats, at $2.99 a month or $14.99 a year with a seven day trial, or $29.99 once.

Does it work offline, and does it collect any data?

It works entirely offline and there is no account. Photo analysis happens on the device and your pictures are never uploaded. Nothing is tracked, there is no advertising, and no data is collected. If you are signed in to iCloud, palettes and folders sync between your own devices through your private database.

What languages does Harmony Palette support?

Eleven: English, German, Spanish, Latin American Spanish, French, Italian, Japanese, Korean, Dutch, Brazilian Portuguese and Simplified Chinese. All 279 color names and all 160 curated palettes are translated too, and search matches both the translated name and the English one.
